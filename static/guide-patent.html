How to file a patent — overview for inventors

Use this path if you made a new and useful invention (machine, process, composition, or improvement). Names and logos are usually trademarks. Books, art, and code are usually copyright.

The path

  1. Document the invention: drawings, how it works, dates of conception, and anyone you already told.
  2. Look around — a prior-art search (USPTO Patent Public Search and published papers) so you know what is already out there.
  3. Choose a first filing. A provisional can lock an early date at lower cost; a non-provisional is examined and can become an issued patent.
  4. File with the USPTO — often with a registered patent attorney or agent. Self-filers still use official forms.
  5. Keep deadlines. A provisional gives you up to 12 months to file a matching non-provisional. Office actions have their own clocks.

Stop and talk to an attorney if…

  • You already showed the invention at a trade show, on a website, or to investors.
  • Someone wants you to assign or license rights, or you have a co-inventor dispute.
  • You received a USPTO office action or a deadline inside six months.
  • The invention is a medical device, software with a business method, or anything you are unsure is even patent-eligible.
