Provisional vs. non-provisional

Educational explainer by Texas IP Law Group. Not legal advice for your facts.

A provisional application can establish an early U.S. filing date. It is not examined, does not become a patent by itself, and expires at 12 months unless you file a matching non-provisional that claims its benefit.

A non-provisional (utility) application is examined. Claims get written, the examiner searches, and you may receive office actions.

Inventors often ask about provisionals before an investor meeting. Timing around public disclosure is fact-specific — this article cannot tell you whether your demo last month still leaves a path. Use the patent guide and official USPTO provisional page ↗, then consult if the date is close.
