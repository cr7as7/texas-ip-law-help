How to file a copyright — overview

Copyright protects original works of authorship fixed in a tangible medium — software, writings, art, music, videos. Registration is with the U.S. Copyright Office, not the USPTO.

The path

  1. Confirm the work is original and already written down, recorded, or saved (not just an idea in your head).
  2. Understand what registration adds: public record, and important timing if you later need to sue.
  3. Prepare the application and deposit copies per Copyright Office rules for your type of work.
  4. File online through copyright.gov (eCO) and track the claim.

Stop and talk to an attorney if…

  • Someone copied your work, or a platform took it down.
  • You need work-for-hire, assignment, or a software license drafted.
  • The work is mixed with a patentable invention or a brand you also want to register.
